Working From Home Deductions: What's Changed Since 2023–24

Updated 23 November 2026 2 min read By the Tax Solutions Group team

Draft note: rewritten 23 November 2026 from the old taxsolutionsgroup.com.au article (first published 7 December 2023). Figures checked against ATO rules on that date. Dmitry to read and approve before launch.

Working from home deductions have changed several times in the last few years. If you’re lodging an older year, catching up on late tax returns, or wondering whether you missed out, here’s what applied when.

For how to claim this year, see our work from home deductions guide.

The rates year by year

Income year Fixed rate per hour What else to know
2022–23 67c New method from 1 July 2022. From 1 March 2023, a record of every hour worked from home was required.
2023–24 67c Full-year record of actual hours required.
2024–25 70c Rate rose to 70c. Same record rules.
2025–26 70c Same rate and records.
2026–27 Check with us (the ATO confirms the rate for each year) New option: a $1,000 standard deduction for work-related expenses, no receipts needed.

In every year, the fixed rate covers energy, phone, internet, stationery and computer consumables. Equipment such as a desk, chair or laptop is claimed separately.

What changed in 2023–24

Two things made 2023–24 different from earlier years:

  1. No more estimates of hours. You needed a record of the actual hours you worked from home for the whole year, such as timesheets, rosters or a diary. A typical four-week period wasn’t enough.
  2. Proof you paid the bills. You needed at least one document for each type of expense the rate covers, like a phone bill or an electricity bill in your name.

Many people under-claimed or skipped the claim that year because the rules were new.

Did you miss out? You may be able to amend

If you didn’t claim working from home expenses, or claimed too little, you can usually amend a return within two years of getting your notice of assessment. For many people, that means 2024–25 is still open, and some 2023–24 returns lodged late in 2024 may still be in time.

You’ll need the same records as for an original claim, especially your hours. If you have them, we can lodge an amendment for you.

What’s new for 2026–27

From 1 July 2026, employees can choose a $1,000 standard deduction for work-related expenses instead of claiming their actual costs. You don’t need receipts, and you don’t need to have spent the money. If your real work expenses are higher, you can still claim them with records. You get one or the other, not both.

For many people who work from home a day or two a week, the $1,000 will be simpler and similar in value. If you work from home most days, or buy expensive equipment, keep tracking your hours.
