Earning Under $18,200? How to Get Your Tax Back

Updated 23 November 2026 3 min read By the Tax Solutions Group team

Draft note: rewritten 23 November 2026 from the old taxsolutionsgroup.com.au article (first published 6 October 2023). Figures checked against ATO rules on that date. Dmitry to read and approve before launch.

  • Fee-from-refund wording: confirm the process with Dmitry (same open item as the FAQ).

Students, part-timers, people who started work late in the year, retirees with a small job: if you earned less than $18,200, you’re under the tax-free threshold. If tax came out of your pay anyway, you can usually get all of it back. But only if you lodge a tax return.

The tax-free threshold in one paragraph

If you’re an Australian resident for tax purposes, the first $18,200 of your taxable income each year is tax-free. With the low income tax offset added, you don’t pay any income tax until your taxable income passes about $22,575 (2025–26). From 2026–27 that rises to about $22,866. Low earners also pay a reduced Medicare levy, or none at all.

Why was tax taken out if I earned under $18,200?

Your employer doesn’t know what you’ll earn over the whole year. Tax gets withheld when:

  • you didn’t claim the tax-free threshold on your TFN declaration
  • you had two jobs, and you can only claim the threshold with one, so the second withholds at a higher rate
  • you earned more in some weeks, for example over Christmas, and withholding is worked out per pay
  • you only worked part of the year, so your annual income ended up lower than your weekly pay suggested.

All of that is fine. The tax return evens it out.

Do I have to lodge?

You need to lodge if any tax was withheld from your pay, even $1. That’s how you get it back.

You may not need to lodge if your income was under $18,200 and no tax was withheld. In that case, send the ATO a non-lodgment advice so it doesn’t think a return is missing. You’ll usually need to lodge anyway if you:

  • had an ABN or business income
  • had income from overseas, rental property, or sold shares or crypto
  • had reportable fringe benefits or super contributions on your income statement.

Not sure? The ATO’s “Do I need to lodge?” tool on its website gives a quick answer, or we can check for you.

Who doesn’t get the tax-free threshold?

  • Foreign residents for tax purposes are taxed from the first dollar.
  • Working holiday makers (417 and 462 visas) pay 15% on income up to $45,000.
  • Under-18s pay special higher rates on unearned income like interest or dividends. Wages from a job are taxed normally.

International students studying here for more than six months are usually residents. See our guide for international students.

Other reasons to lodge on a low income

  • The super co-contribution. If you put some of your own after-tax money into super, the government may add up to $500. It’s worked out from your lodged tax return.
  • Centrelink and family payments are based on your lodged income.
  • Old years. If you’ve been on a low income for years and never lodged, there may be refunds waiting. See how late you can lodge.

Is a tax agent worth it on a low income?

You can lodge yourself through myTax for free. A registered tax agent is worth it if you have more than one job, deductions you’re unsure about, missing years, or you just want it done right. Check that any agent you use is on the Tax Practitioners Board register. Their fee is tax-deductible next year.
